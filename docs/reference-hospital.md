# The reference hospital

BillingRecon has no client yet (the owner, 2026-11-25). Phase 10 was written for "the client": six inputs were to come
from a hospital before its prompts could start. Until a real hospital exists, this document stands in for it. It
describes one **fictional** hospital, decided by Claude Code at the owner's request, of the kind R1 serves best, and
gives each of the six inputs of `docs/build-prompts.md`, Phase 10 (ADR 0058).

**Nothing here is real.** No person, address, phone number, registration or logo below belongs to anyone. Every record
made from this document is synthetic, so rule 6 of the bar of Phase 10 (no real patient's data outside production)
holds by construction. When a real hospital comes, its own inputs replace this document, and the lists under
"What a real hospital changes" say what to look at again.

## 1. The hospital

| | |
|---|---|
| Name | Northfield Care Hospital (fictional) |
| Code, as typed at sign-in | `northfield` |
| Kind | Multi-specialty, about 120 beds, outpatient and inpatient, one site |
| Payers | Self-pay only: the patient or a guarantor. No insurer, TPA or corporate account (R1 has none; R4) |
| Volume | About 250 bills and 300 payments a working day, 15 staff on the billing and cash desks over two shifts |
| Fiscal year | 1 April to 31 March |
| Settings (BR-607) | Back-dating 7 days, bills due after 15 days, a bill of ₹1,00,000 or more needs approval |
| Prefixes (BR-802) | The standard ones: `BIL`, `CN`, `PAY`, `ADJ`, `REF` |

Why this kind: R1 is complete for self-pay billing and has nothing for claims, so a self-pay hospital can use all of it
and misses nothing; and its volume is the one the load test of `docs/requirements.md` section 5 was sized for.

## 2. The six inputs

### Input 1: the export of the old system, and the cut-over

- **Cut-over date: 2027-04-01**, the first day of fiscal year 2027-28. Opening balances are as at the close of
  2027-03-31; the first bill raised in BillingRecon is dated 2027-04-01 or later.
- **The export is synthetic,** generated by a script of the repository from a fixed seed, never copied from anywhere:
  2,000 patients with their payers, 12 guarantors who are not patients, 300 unpaid bills, 40 advances not yet applied,
  15 credit balances, and a trial balance of the three totals that agrees with the rows to the paisa
  (`deploy/reference/export/`, written by `deploy/reference/generate-export.mjs`). The export itself is clean, so that
  it imports; the faults a real export has (two patients with one MRN, a bill for a patient who is not in the file, a
  row with no name, an amount with three decimals, a date after the cut-over) are in the tests of the import.
- **Its layout** is plain CSV, UTF-8, one file per kind of record, as `docs/runbook.md` section 18.1 defines it. A real hospital's export is mapped to that layout before it is imported; the import does not read another
  system's format.

### Input 2: the documents, and what they must show

- A **bill** is printed as a *bill of supply* when none of its lines carries tax, and as a *tax invoice* when one does.
  Healthcare services by a clinical establishment are taken as exempt from GST; a line is taxable only when its service
  item carries a tax rate.
- A **receipt** for a payment and a **refund voucher** for a refund, in the same style.
- Each shows the letterhead of input 6, the document's gapless number and date, the patient with MRN, the payer, the
  lines, the totals, the amount in words, and the signatory.
- **This is Claude Code's reading, not advice.** Which document a hospital must issue, and what it must show, is its
  chartered accountant's decision (`docs/go-live.md`, when written, carries it as an open sign-off).

### Input 3: where production runs

Not decided here: it costs money and makes accounts, which are the owner's. Until the owner decides, Prompt 10.5 is
written for any Linux host with Docker and a domain the owner controls, with the secrets in that host's own store, and
the deployment itself stays owed.

### Input 4: self-pay only

Yes, by definition of the hospital above.

### Input 5: the staff

Fifteen people, all fictional, at `@northfield.example` (a domain reserved for examples, which reaches nobody):

| Role | People |
|---|---|
| TenantAdmin | 2 (the accounts manager and the IT lead) |
| Supervisor | 3 (one per shift, one finance) |
| BillingClerk | 5 |
| Cashier | 4 |
| Viewer | 1 (the auditor) |

Two administrators and three supervisors, so that every maker-checker decision has a second person on each shift
(BR-603) and the hospital is never left with one administrator (ADR 0030).

### Input 6: the letterhead

| | |
|---|---|
| Legal name | Northfield Care Hospital Private Limited (fictional) |
| Address | 12 Example Road, Sector 4, Sample Nagar, Pune, Maharashtra |
| GSTIN | A placeholder that is not a registration, printed as `GSTIN: (not registered: reference hospital)` |
| Logo | A mark drawn for this document, in the tokens of the theme; nobody's trademark |
| Signatory | "Authorised signatory", with no name |

## 3. What a real hospital changes

- Its export has its own layout, duplicates and gaps: map it to the import's layout, run the dry run, and read what it
  rejects before deciding anything.
- Its accountant decides the tax document and its wording.
- Its cut-over date, settings, prefixes, staff and letterhead replace the ones above.
- If it bills insurers, R1 is not enough (R4).
- The legal and security steps cannot be stood in for: the DPDP review, the data processing agreement, the penetration
  test, the hospital's own acceptance.
